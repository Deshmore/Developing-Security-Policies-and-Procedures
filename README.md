Laboratory Scenario

Welcome to NexusTech Solutions, a rapidly growing mid-sized software development company specialising in cloud-based enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems. The organisation has expanded from 50 to 250 employees and has recently acquired high-profile clients in the financial and healthcare sectors.
You have been appointed Information Security Manager and have discovered that the company still relies on a five-year-old “IT Rules” document that mixes high-level policy intent, technical configuration instructions and vague recommendations. This has created inconsistent practices, unclear accountability and compliance gaps.
CEO Marcus Vance has directed you to establish a formal, structured and maintainable information security policy framework. Your mission is to build the foundation using the Security Policy Development Lifecycle so that NexusTech can strengthen governance, support ISO/IEC 27001 and SOC 2 readiness, and protect its growing information assets.

Your role
Act as the Information Security Manager. Every artefact you produce should be suitable for management review and realistic organisational use, not merely a theoretical answer.

Learning Objectives
•	Structure a security policy hierarchy covering Policies, Standards, Guidelines and Procedures.
•	Apply the Security Policy Development Lifecycle to a realistic organisational context.
•	Write clear, concise and enforceable security policy statements.
•	Develop actionable procedures that operationalise policy requirements.
•	Design policy communication, implementation, exception, enforcement and review mechanisms.
•	Demonstrate professional governance judgement through evidence-based policy decisions.



Assignment Overview

ICDFA is strengthening its information security governance framework and requires a coherent, professional and implementable security policy manual. You have been appointed as an Information Security Policy Adviser and are responsible for translating governance objectives into clear organisational rules, responsibilities, procedures and measurable controls.
Your task is to develop a structured Security Policy Manual for ICDFA that demonstrates the relationship between policy, standard, procedure and guideline; defines practical security requirements; assigns ownership and accountability; aligns key requirements with recognised security frameworks; and establishes a lifecycle for approval, communication, exceptions, review and continuous improvement.

Learning Outcomes
•	Distinguish and correctly apply the hierarchy of policy, standard, procedure and guideline.
•	Develop clear, enforceable and business-aligned information security policies.
•	Define ownership, accountability, enforcement and exception processes.
•	Translate policy requirements into supporting procedures and operational expectations.
•	Map key policy requirements to ISO/IEC 27001 control themes and NIST Cybersecurity Framework functions.
•	Design policy communication, training, monitoring, review and continuous-improvement mechanisms.
•	Develop meaningful metrics for measuring policy adoption, compliance and effectiveness.

Scenario

ICDFA has expanded its academic, cloud, laboratory and digital service environments. As its systems, users, partners and learning platforms grow, security requirements can no longer depend on informal practices or isolated technical decisions. Senior management has therefore authorised the creation of a formal Security Policy Manual that will provide consistent direction across personnel, systems, data, cloud services, laboratories and physical facilities.
As the assigned policy adviser, you must create a policy package that is practical enough to implement, clear enough for users to understand and structured enough to support governance oversight, auditability and continuous improvement.
